#63. NZ election 2026: New Zealand faces weeks of uncertainty with results ‘too close to call’

National party will be largest in parliament but ultimate shape of next government won’t be settled for some time

New Zealand could be facing a weeks-long wait to know the shape of its next government, after its nail-biting election failed to hand a decisive victory to either the incumbent rightwing coalition or opposition left bloc.

On preliminary results, the centre-right National party, led by the prime minister, Christopher Luxon, managed to secure enough seats to govern alongside its traditional coalition partners, the libertarian Act and populist New Zealand First parties.

But with some seats won by very slim margins, and special votes yet to be counted, that success is precarious, resulting in an election too close to call until official results are released on 27 November.

Special votes include overseas votes and ballots cast outside a voter’s electorate. In 2023, 600,000 such votes were made, 21% of the total. These votes tend to favour the left – in 2023, special votes resulted in Te Pāti Māori (the Māori party) and the Greens gaining three seats between them.

With nearly 100% of the regular votes counted on Saturday night, National secured 42 seats, Act 13 and New Zealand First 12. The combined 67-seat majority is wafer thin, with the left bloc just three seats behind.

Speaking to a room full of the party faithful at Auckland’s Viaduct events centre just before midnight, Luxon said the results were “too close to call” and the special votes would need to be counted before negotiations could begin.

National was still the largest party in parliament, he said, and had “a pathway to forming the next government”. National pipped Labour by 0.02% in the party vote.

Labour, led by Chris Hipkins, secured 35 seats, while its traditional coalition partners the Greens won 16 and Te Pāti Māori five. Even with the seven seats from the centrist newcomer party, Opportunity, the left bloc is three seats short of forming a government.

Before a rowdy crowd of supporters at the Cosmopolitan Club in Upper Hutt, north of Wellington, Hipkins joked that New Zealand had spoken but “it may take a bit longer to work out what they’ve said”.

“As things stand tonight, it is clear that we are not going to know the shape of our next government for a little while longer yet,” Hipkins said, promising that Labour would get “straight to work” should the final count land in its favour.

The collapse of the two major parties, amid a sluggish economy and stubbornly high ⁠inflation, is feeding record support for the smaller parties, including Act, New Zealand First and the Greens. It is also helped propel Opportunity into parliament for the first time.

Opinion polls in the lead-up to the election had Opportunity placed to become the kingmaker. At this stage, the right bloc does not need the party to govern, but if special votes change the landscape, its predicted role could come to fruition.

Currently at 5.9%, the centrist party has met the threshold needed to enter parliament, a historic first. Since New Zealand adopted the mixed-member proportional (MMP) electoral system in 1996, no new party has been able to meet that threshold – all the other minor parties have been born from MPs or groups splintering from larger parties.

The party’s leader, Qiulae Wong, told her supporters in Auckland she was “stoked”.

“It’s looking very promising for us,” she said. “Let’s just see how the rest of the votes fall.”

In New Zealand, voters cast two votes: one for an electorate MP and one for a party. The system is meant to produce a parliament that reflects each party’s share of the nationwide vote.

However, if New Zealanders vote for more electorate MPs from a particular party than their party vote would entitle them to, they get to keep the extra MPs in what is called an overhang.

National won more seats that its party support allows for, meaning it could gain an extra five seats to make up the balance.

That will be good news for its path to power, but comes with some significant trade-offs for the party. Four of its most senior ministers, including Nicola Willis, Paul Goldsmith, Chris Bishop and Gerry Brownlee, will not return to parliament because they did not win a seat, or the party didn’t win enough votes to bring in list-only MPs.

On the other side of the house, Te Pāti Māori picked up five overhang seats.

With all the extra seats, there could be 130 in the next parliament – a record under MMP – which means 65 seats are needed to form a coalition.

The country faces a two-week wait until special votes are announced, followed by the possibility of weeks of negotiations. Luxon said caretaker government conventions would apply in the meantime.

“For now, I want to tell New Zealanders that well-established caretaker conventions apply … The daily business of government will continue.”

The girlfriend of one of the men said her boyfriend, whose name is being withheld for security reasons, told the Guardian he wanted to go “anywhere else but China”.

Human rights groups said two Uyghur men were at risk of deportation after recently being transferred to an immigration detention centre and they feared Thai authorities may once again give in to pressure from Beijing.

The men had been serving a seven-year prison sentence for offences related to an attempted escape from immigration detention in January 2020. They were part of a group of more than 200 Uyghur men, women and children who were apprehended by Thai police in 2014 near the border with Malaysia after they fled from China.

Since 2014, women and children in the group have been released to Turkey, while the men have been deported to China, in separate batches, attracting criticism from the United Nations and the US secretary of state, Marco Rubio.

Uyghurs, a Turkic minority mainly from the Xinjiang region in China, have been subjected to decades of oppressive and discriminatory treatment from the Chinese authorities. It is not clear whether China has issued a formal extradition request for the two men. But Chaipruak Doungprapat, Thailand’s national security council chief, told reporters that the men, along with three other Uyghurs who are still in prison, had arrest warrants outstanding in China.

#57. Makers of GLP-1 drugs ordered to add warnings about rare eye condition that can lead to blindness

The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) told makers of popular weight-loss GLP-1 drugs that they must add warning labels about a rare eye condition that can cause sudden blindness.

Manufacturers of Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound must warn about nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION). The disorder happens when vessels supplying blood to the optic nerves become blocked, depriving the nerves of oxygen.

The condition can cause painless decreased vision in one eye, which can be permanent, the FDA said.

In a statement to the BBC, the American Academy of Ophthalmology said "any added risk appears small, and it has not been established that these medications cause NAION".

"GLP-1 medications offer important benefits for people with diabetes, obesity, and heart disease, so our message to patients is simple," the group said. "Don't stop your medication without talking to your doctor, and if you notice sudden, painless vision loss in one eye, see an ophthalmologist promptly."

There is no known treatment to reverse vision loss caused by NAION. It is the most common cause of acute optic nerve injury in people over 50 years old, according to the academy.

The FDA said it conducted a detailed review of reports of NAION in GLP-1 users as well as a review of other studies and concluded there is an "increased risk of rare NAION events" in people taking the drugs.

In a statement, Novo Nordisk, the manufacturer of Wegovy, one of the most popular weight-loss medications, said the company believes the data "do not establish a cause-and-effect relationship".

"We recognize that NAION can have a profound impact on patients and their families. Patient safety is a top priority for Novo, and we continue to monitor the safety of our medicines," the manufacturer said.

Some research was challenging to review because people taking GLP-1 drugs for obesity and type 2 diabetes may already have risk factors for NAION separate from the drugs, according to the regulator. The FDA said it was not known why those taking GLP-1 drugs may be more at risk for the eye condition.

The drugs already have warning labels for several other conditions including pancreatitis and gallbladder disease. Some of the most common symptoms for semaglutide - a key ingredient in many GLP-1 drugs - include nausea, vomiting and stomach pain.

Lawyers for former President Donald Trump told a New York judge on Monday that they oppose the request by media outlets to broadcast Trump’s arraignment on Tuesday.

“We submit that the media request should be denied because it will create a circus-like atmosphere at the arraignment, raise unique security concerns, and is inconsistent with President Trump’s presumption of innocence,” the Trump team said in a letter to New York Supreme Court Judge Juan Merchant.

Trump’s attorneys said in their letter to Acting New York Supreme Court Judge Juan Merchan that he is required under court rules to take into account potential security concerns.

“As Your Honor is well-aware, this case presents extraordinary security concerns (including Secret Service-related concerns) and we submit that any video or photography of the proceedings will only heighten these serious concerns,” they wrote.

Among the Trump lawyers signing the new letter was Todd Blanche, who, CNN reported, recently joined the former president’s legal team.

Meanwhile, the Manhattan District Attorney’s office also responded Monday to the news outlets’ request, and said that it was deferring to the judge to decide how best to manage his court room.

However, the district attorney’s office wrote that it would “be a defensible exercise of the Court’s discretion to exclude or restrict videography, photography, and radio coverage of the arraignment in the interest of avoiding potential prejudice to the defendant, maintaining an orderly proceeding, assuring the safety of the participants in the proceeding, or for other reasons within the Court’s broad authority to manage and control these proceedings.”

But the prosecutors also noted that there “does not appear to be a categorical prohibition on cameras during an arraignment” under existing New York statutes and case law.

While the district attorney’s office stopped short of opposing the request, its lawyer pointed out to Merchan that a similar request for audio-video broadcasting was made for the 2021 arraignment in the tax fraud case against Trump Organization and its CFO Allen Weisselberg.

The judge responded to that 2021 request by “allowing a limited number of still photographs to be taken prior to the commencement of proceedings,” the prosecutors noted Monday.

#2. Lewis Hamilton visits Egypt’s Rafah crossing and Gaza aid facilities

Seven-time F1 world champion tours border area and logistics warehouses operated by Egyptian Red Crescent

Lewis Hamilton has visited Egypt’s Rafah border crossing with the Gaza Strip to meet organisations sending humanitarian aid to the territory.

The seven-time Formula One world champion has spoken out about conflict and humanitarian crises in the region, and has called for peace. He has frequently posted on social media about children killed in Israeli airstrikes in Gaza and called for a ceasefire.

“I think we need to make sure we’re pushing to free Congo, free Sudan, free Syria, free Palestine. I think there’s a lot of peace that I’m hoping we get to at some stage,” Hamilton said in October.

A ceasefire agreement reached last year has halted major combat operations in Gaza, but the amount of aid reaching the territory continues to fall far short of the agreed-upon minimum of 600 trucks a day.

Israeli forces have continued to advance, expanding their control from 53% of the territory to at least 60%, an independent commission of UN experts said last month.

Airstrikes also continue, and Israeli fire along the advancing front has killed at least 196 Palestinians, including 18 women, 32 boys and 11 girls, the commission said.

Hamilton toured Egypt’s Rafah border crossing with Gaza before visiting logistics warehouses operated by the Egyptian Red Crescent, which facilitates the entry of aid to Gaza.

At the crossing, Hamilton greeted aid workers with “Assalamu alaikum” a widely used Arabic greeting meaning “peace be upon you.” He shook hands and briefly spoke to workers and officials.

Khaled Megawer, the governor of North Sinai, where Rafah is located, said the visit was intended to help Hamilton learn about Egypt’s efforts to send aid to Gaza.

The visit comes after Hamilton made his 400th race start on Sunday at the São Paulo Grand Prix at Interlagos.

The first black F1 driver, he made his debut with McLaren in 2007. He is only the second F1 driver to start 400 races after his former teammate Fernando Alonso, who achieved the milestone in 2024.
