#57. Nine injured, three critically, after Bangkok oil refinery explosion

Nine people have been injured after an explosion at an oil refinery in Bangkok, authorities have said.

Three of them are in critical condition, according to news agency AFP, which said the local hospital had revised its earlier assessment of four.

A fire broke out after the blast at the Phra Khanong facility run by energy giant Bangchak Corporation in the east of Thailand's capital at around 14:45 (07:45 GMT).

The company said the fire was brought under control and put out within 30 minutes, adding that the cause of the incident was being investigated.

Black smoke could be seen across Bangkok's skyline after the blast, as local officials told residents to leave the area.

An alert sent to mobile phones by Bangkok authorities confirmed that the fire was under control, but urged people to avoid the area.

Eyewitnesses shared video clips on social media of flames and smoke rising above the refinery.

On a visit to the scene, Thailand's energy minister Akanat Promphan said it appeared the impact of the explosion was limited because the fire started in a distillation tower about 100m (330ft) from where oil was being stored.

He said the incident was concerning as the refinery was in a residential area and the government was suspending production until it could be confident that operations would resume without any issues.

Bangchak Corporation said air quality monitoring in the immediate area around the refinery was within normal ranges.

It confirmed that production at the site, which can process up to 120,000 barrels of oil a day had been shut down. The company has a second refinery in Chonburi province, about 50 miles (80km) from Bangkok, handling a daily capacity of about 180,000 barrels.

An explosion occurred at the Bangkok refinery in July 2012, sparking a fire but no reported injuries.

She said he faced the last few years "with so much courage and determination" and said they were "grateful" for the "kindness and support shown to him by fans throughout his illness", saying it brought him "immense comfort and strength".

The son of a fisherman, Taylor taught himself guitar, bass, and drums and began playing professionally at age 13.

He played with several bands around the UK before answering a music paper advertisement for a "live-wire guitarist", which led to his audition for Duran Duran in Birmingham in April 1980.

The group went on to achieve global fame through the New Romantic movement, which combined slick, melodic synth-pop with aspirational music videos and chiselled good looks.

Taylor's guitar lines - full of glassy, powerful riffs - were often a highlight of their songs, from the choppy opening of Girls on Film to the sleazy scuzz of Hungry Like The Wolf.

Duran Duran bridged the sounds of pop, funk, disco, punk and new wave with a sound that remains indelibly tied to the 1980s

Duran Duran were one of the biggest pop acts of the 1980s, and its five members became global hearththrobs.

Their indelible hooks and airbrushed music videos found a natural home on the newly launched MTV, giving their career a huge boost in the US.

They became so popular in the US that the press labeled them the leaders of a "second British invasion" of the charts - alongside groups like Spandau Ballet, Human League and Eurythmics.

During a hiatus in 1984, Andy and John Taylor enlisted Robert Palmer for their successful but short-lived side project the Power Station.

The following year, the guitarist decided to quit Duran Duran after a miserable experience performing at Live Aid.

"We'd reached rock bottom and we had absolutely nothing to say to each other," he wrote in his autobiography, Wild Boy.

"We drove to the venue through streets packed with excited rock fans, but inside the bus the atmosphere was as if we were on a way to a funeral."

He went on to play on Palmer's Addicted To Love, and released a solo album, Thunder, in 1987.

Taylor quit Duran Duran at the height of their fame in 1985, but rejoined the band several times in the 2000s and contributed to their most recent album, Danse Macabre

After a period writing and producing albums for other artists, he rejoined Duran Duran in 2000, lasting for just one album (2004's Astronaut) before parting ways again.

In 2022, when the band were inducted to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, Taylor was notably absent from the ceremony, despite public plans for a reunion.

When the group took the stage, Le Bon read a letter from the guitarist, in which he revealed his diagnosis with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer.

Nonetheless, he contributed to the following year's Halloween-themed album Danse Macabre, marking his final collaboration with the band.

#48. Myanmar refuses to comply with parts of ICJ genocide ruling

Myanmar's president Min Aung Hlaing has led the country since he seized power in a 2021 military coup

Myanmar will not comply with "inappropriate" parts of the genocide ruling by the International Court of Justice (ICJ), its leader Min Aung Hlaing has said in his first public comments since the court decision.

The ICJ's landmark ruling last week found Myanmar guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya minority in a 2017 military crackdown that killed thousands and forced more than 700,000 to flee.

Myanmar denied committing genocide, saying its forces had responded to attacks by armed groups. It is the first state to have been found in violation of the 1948 Genocide Convention.

Min Aung Hlaing said "the term 'genocide' had seriously affected the country's reputation", state media report.

"Myanmar would take actions appropriate for a responsible country but would not comply with requirements it considered inappropriate," the country's coup leader-turned-president said at a meeting on Wednesday, according to state media.

The statement did not specify which parts of the ruling the country objects to.

The genocide case, brought by The Gambia in 2019, centred around so-called "clearance operations" initiated in 2017 in Rakhine State, where many of the Rohingya Muslim minority resided.

The ICJ determined that about 10,000 Rohingya were violently killed during the operation, while more than 700,000 are thought to have fled to Bangladesh by foot or by sea. The court found that approximately 815,000 had been targeted in some way.

The verdict was reached by a majority of nine votes to six among a panel of judges from around the world.

The ICJ also found in favour by majority that Myanmar had violated several other parts of the 1948 Genocide Convention.

The ruling is final and cannot be appealed.

The ICJ ordered Myanmar to pay proportionate reparations, but said an exact figure should be determined at a later date.

The court also ordered Myanmar to stop ongoing violations and immediately investigate those suspected of breaking the Convention.

But the ICJ does not have the power to enforce these demands. Instead it has to rely on the UN Security Council and political pressure to make its rulings stick.

While the ruling was largely symbolic, it was welcomed by Rohingya communities and human rights groups who have long called attention to the suffering faced by the Rohingya.
